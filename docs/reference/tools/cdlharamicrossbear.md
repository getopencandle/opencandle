## Purpose
A harami whose second bar is a doji, after an advance.

## How the author meant it to be used
Mirror of the bullish harami cross: a long white bar, then a doji inside its body, in an uptrend (Nison; ChartSchool). A top warning. Nison's wording is paraphrased here.

## How you interact with it
Automatic. Nothing is placed or anchored by you. You choose the pattern direction (bullish, bearish or both) and how the prior trend is detected (price against a 50-bar average, 50 and 200-bar averages, or no detection). The mark is drawn on the bar that completes the pattern and is known only at that bar's close, so on the live bar it can appear or vanish until the bar closes. Once the bar is closed it does not repaint and never uses later bars. The mark sits on the second bar at its close.

## Calculation
Bar 1 white and long, bar 2 a doji inside its body, trend up. TA-Lib returns -100 for a white first bar. TA-Lib measures "long", "short", "doji", "very short shadow", "near" and "equal" against averages of the previous bars: body long or short = against the average real body of the previous 10 bars; doji = body at most 10% of the high-low range averaged over 10 bars; very short shadow = under 10% of the range average; near = 20% of range (5-bar average); equal = 5% of range (5-bar average). Marks need those prior bars, so none appear on the first bars of a chart.

## Standard defaults
As the bullish harami cross.

## Reading it
Top warning.

## Common mistakes and edge cases
- As the bullish harami cross.

## How OpenCandle does it
- Bar 2 is a doji (body at most 5% of its own range; TA-Lib uses 10% of the average range) inside bar 1's body. Touching edges count as inside, and bar 1 must be a long white body.
- Needs a prior uptrend.
- Trend is judged on the bar before the pattern starts, using the close against its 50-bar average by default (or the 50 above the 200, or no trend test).
- The mark appears when the last bar closes. OpenCandle does not wait for follow-through, so confirmation is up to you.
