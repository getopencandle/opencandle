## Purpose
Labels a double three, a sideways correction made of two simple corrections (W and Y) joined by a connecting wave X.

## How the author meant it to be used
In Elliott's scheme, when a simple correction does not finish the job, the market can chain a second one after a connecting wave X, giving W-X-Y (a double three). From Frost and Prechter: W and Y are each a zigzag, flat or (for Y) a triangle, and X is usually a small corrective wave that retraces part of W; only one zigzag can appear in a combination and a triangle can appear only as the last part. The structure is sideways overall. Treat all of this as general guidance.

## How you draw it
Four clicks in time order: an unlabelled origin (the start of W), then the ends of W, X and Y. All four are draggable. Labels W, X, Y sit on the points. Legs are straight lines. Anchored in time and price; stays on its bars across interval changes. Nothing is checked by the drawing.

## Geometry and levels
X retraces part of W (often less than a full retracement); Y usually ends near the end of W or beyond it. These ratios are not firm rules.

## Standard defaults
Labels W, X, Y. No levels.

## Reading it
It marks a complex correction, after which the previous trend usually resumes. Counts are revised as price evolves.

## Common mistakes and edge cases
- Using it for an impulse or a single three-wave correction.
- Mixing W and Y types in a way the guidelines forbid.
- Labelling X as a trend leg rather than a connecting wave.

## How OpenCandle does it
- An unlabelled origin plus W, X, Y. Labels are plain, with no rule checks.
