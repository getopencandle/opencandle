## Purpose
Labels a triple three, the longest sideways correction: three simple corrections (W, Y and Z) joined by two connecting waves X.

## How the author meant it to be used
Elliott allowed a combination to be extended to a triple three, W-X-Y-X-Z, when a double three does not finish the correction. From Frost and Prechter: it is rare, W, Y and Z are simple corrections (zigzag, flat or triangle with the triangle only last), and each X connects two of them. Treat all of this as general guidance.

## How you draw it
Six clicks in time order: an unlabelled origin (the start of W), then the ends of W, X, Y, X and Z. The two X points carry the same label. All six are draggable. Legs are straight lines. Anchored in time and price; stays on its bars across interval changes. Nothing is checked by the drawing.

## Geometry and levels
Each X retraces part of the preceding simple correction; the overall shape stays roughly sideways. No firm ratios.

## Standard defaults
Labels W, X, Y, X, Z. No levels.

## Reading it
It marks an extended, complex correction. Triple threes are rare, so a count of this kind is more likely a mislabelled double three or something larger.

## Common mistakes and edge cases
- Using it for a trend leg.
- Confusing the two X points because they share a label.
- Labelling Z as a new impulse.

## How OpenCandle does it
- An unlabelled origin plus W, X, Y, X, Z (six points; the X label repeats). Labels are plain, with no rule checks.
