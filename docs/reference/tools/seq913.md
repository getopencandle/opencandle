## Purpose
A bar-counting exhaustion tool: a 9-bar setup followed by a 13-bar countdown, flagging potential trend exhaustion and turns.

## How the author meant it to be used
Tom DeMark's TD Sequential (The New Science of Technical Analysis, 1994) looks for stretched trends. In the textbook rules, a price flip starts a setup; nine consecutive qualifying closes complete it; a completed setup is followed by a countdown to 13 qualifying bars; a completed 13 marks possible exhaustion. DeMark's original also has extra qualifiers (for instance the 13th-bar condition and setup trend levels) that OpenCandle does not draw.

## How you interact with it
Automatic overlay of numbers on bars (setup 1 to 9, countdown 1 to 13). You toggle setup, countdown, the first number shown, perfected-9 marks and the two lookbacks. Nothing is placed. Each count uses the current and earlier bars only, so a drawn number never changes; the count on the live bar can still appear or reset until it closes.

## Calculation
Buy setup: a price flip (the prior bar's close above the close four bars earlier, the current close below the close four bars earlier), then nine consecutive closes below the close four bars earlier. Sell setup mirrors it. Perfected buy 9: the low of bar 8 or 9 at or below the lows of bars 6 and 7 (sell uses highs). Buy countdown: after a completed buy setup, count bars (not necessarily consecutive) whose close is at or below the low two bars earlier, up to 13; sell mirrors with highs. A completed opposite setup cancels a running countdown. A bar with a missing close resets the setups. Recycling and the optional 13th-bar qualifier are not implemented.

## Standard defaults
Lookbacks 4 (setup) and 2 (countdown), setup numbers from 1, perfected marks on.

## Reading it
A 9 or 13 is an exhaustion warning, not an entry; DeMark pairs the counts with confirmation. Strong trends can continue through completed counts.

## Common mistakes and edge cases
- Starting a setup without a price flip; the flip is required.
- Counting a countdown bar as consecutive (it need not be).
- Restarting setups on unrelated bars; a setup breaks if a bar fails the condition.
- Missing 13th-bar qualifier or setup-cancellation details differ between implementations.
- Counts differ if the data starts mid-trend.

## How OpenCandle does it
- Requires the price flip to start a setup, then nine consecutive closes versus four bars earlier. A perfected 9 compares the low (high) of bar 8 or 9 with bars 6 and 7.
- The countdown counts bars that need not be consecutive: close at or below the low two bars earlier (sell: at or above the high). A completed opposite setup cancels a running countdown.
- Not included: recycling, the TDST cancellation, and the 13th-bar qualifier (bar 13's low at or below bar 8's close). A strict DeMark count may therefore show a 13 earlier than DeMark's own rules would.

## Open questions
DeMark's original qualifiers and recycling rules are not implemented; this entry is low confidence.
